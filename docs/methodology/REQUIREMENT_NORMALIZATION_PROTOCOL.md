# Governance Requirement Normalization Protocol

## Purpose

The taxonomy converts heterogeneous legal and policy language into reusable
architectural requirement types while preserving complete source lineage.

Normalization does not replace the controlling source.

## Atomicity rule

A source requirement must describe one independently assessable legal,
administrative, technical, procedural, or enforcement proposition.

A single source requirement may map to multiple normalized requirement types
when the source provision performs multiple distinct governance functions.

## Mapping strengths

### DIRECT

The source requirement expressly imposes, prohibits, authorizes, limits, or
creates the normalized governance function.

### RELATED

The source requirement materially supports or implies the governance function
but does not independently establish the full requirement.

No RELATED mapping may later be treated as direct legal equivalence.

## Actor preservation

Every source requirement must retain its original obligated or affected actor.

Normalization must not transform:

- a government duty into a company duty;
- a voluntary activity into a compulsory activity;
- an enforcement authority into a regulated actor;
- a private remedy into an agency mandate; or
- a legal limitation into a positive authorization.

## Modality preservation

The following modalities remain distinct:

- mandatory;
- voluntary;
- conditional;
- prohibited;
- permitted;
- exempted;
- authority-limited;
- liability-creating;
- no-private-right-created.

## Deadline preservation

Source deadlines remain attached to source requirements.

The normalized taxonomy describes requirement type, not the date on which the
requirement becomes operative.

## Evidence lineage

Every mapping must preserve:

1. source record identifier;
2. source requirement identifier;
3. normalized requirement type;
4. mapping strength;
5. written rationale;
6. review status.

## Crosswalk rule

ORYNTH compatibility will be assessed against normalized requirement types and
specific source requirements.

A taxonomy match alone does not establish legal compliance.

## Expansion rule

The taxonomy may expand when a new source obligation cannot be represented
without distortion.

Existing identifiers must not be silently reassigned.
