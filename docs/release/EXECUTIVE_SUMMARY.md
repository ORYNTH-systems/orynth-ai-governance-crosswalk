# Executive Summary

**ORYNTH AI Governance Crosswalk — Version 1.0.0**

The ORYNTH AI Governance Crosswalk provides an evidence-based architectural compatibility assessment between selected United States AI-governance requirements and mechanisms in the public ORYNTH Reference Architecture (v1.0.0) and Architectural Audit Specification (v1.0.0).

This release evaluates **34 atomic requirements** drawn from Executive Order 14409 and Minnesota HF 1606 / Chapter 72.

## Key Findings

The adjudication applies a deterministic, adversarially reviewed methodology to map source requirements against indexed public evidence. The resulting verdict distribution is:

- **PARTIALLY ADDRESSED (9):** Direct bounded architectural support exists, though external duties remain.
- **RELATED (4):** An architecturally relevant mechanism exists but does not perform the central duty.
- **NOT APPLICABLE (8):** The requirement falls outside declared architectural scope (e.g., establishing government agencies, penalties, or statutory authority).
- **GAP (13):** The requirement is architecturally relevant, but no qualifying public ORYNTH mechanism currently addresses it.

**Zero requirements are claimed as fully ADDRESSED.** This is a deliberate trust signal. The adjudication reports architectural correspondence only where the public evidence strictly supports it, refusing to round partial support up to full support or manufacture coverage.

The **13 gaps** are preserved as a valid research result. They concentrate in federal program operation, government implementation, vulnerability management, and enforcement functions that the public architecture does not perform. No private, unpublished, or separate-ecosystem architecture is used to conceal these gaps.

## Evidence and Traceability

Every verdict traces directly to a primary government record. The adjudication used **24 of 40** indexed public evidence records, establishing **66 requirement-to-evidence relationships**. Furthermore, the matrix records **32 residual duties**, preserving external legal, governmental, and operational responsibilities that remain outside the architecture.

## Required Claim Boundary

This crosswalk is an architectural compatibility assessment. **It is not legal advice.**

It does not establish legal or regulatory compliance, government approval, or regulatory certification. It does not certify independent implementations, nor does it establish or guarantee safety or correctness. Architectural correspondence, legal compliance, and governmental authority are kept strictly distinct.
