# State Legislative Concentration — Preliminary Findings

## National acceleration

The available national record demonstrates rapid expansion:

- In 2023, at least 25 states introduced AI legislation.
- In 2024, at least 45 states introduced AI legislation.
- In 2025, all 50 states introduced AI measures.
- More than 1,000 AI-related measures were introduced nationally during 2025.

The change is not merely numerical.

The subject matter moved from studies and government inventories toward
private-sector duties, model evaluation, civil-rights protections,
provenance, prohibited capabilities, cybersecurity, and frontier-model
governance.

## Selected structural centers

Within the current enacted-law backbone:

### Colorado

Represents comprehensive high-risk-system governance through duties assigned
to developers and deployers.

### Texas

Represents broad prohibited-practice governance combined with centralized
attorney-general enforcement and a regulatory sandbox.

### California

Represents generative-content transparency, detection, and provenance.

### Utah

Represents disclosure obligations combined with regulatory learning and
controlled experimentation.

### Illinois

Represents sector-specific employment and discrimination governance.

### Tennessee

Represents voice, likeness, image, and digital-replica protection.

### Minnesota

Represents direct capability prohibition with substantial public and private
enforcement.

## Required limitation

These findings identify structurally distinct governance models.

They do not yet establish which state introduced the numerically greatest
number of AI bills.

That question requires complete database ingestion, companion-bill
deduplication, status normalization, and topic-boundary control.
