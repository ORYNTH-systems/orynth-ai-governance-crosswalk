# Minnesota HF 1606 — Final-Law Analysis

## Final status

Minnesota HF 1606 became Minnesota Session Laws 2026, Chapter 72.

The law creates Minnesota Statutes section 325E.91 and becomes effective
August 1, 2026.

## Directly regulated actors

The statute applies to a person who owns or controls:

- a website;
- an application;
- software;
- a program; or
- another service.

The law is capability-based rather than industry-label-based.

An entity does not need to describe itself as an artificial-intelligence
company to fall within the statutory language.

## Prohibited conduct

A covered person must not:

1. allow a user to access, download, or use a covered service to nudify an
   image or video;
2. nudify an image or video on behalf of a user; or
3. advertise or promote a service performing those actions.

## Technical-skill exemption

The prohibition does not apply where the service requires a user's technical
skill to perform the alteration.

Technical skill requires substantial individualized technological or artistic
skill and judgment by a human creator.

## Civil enforcement

An individual depicted in unlawfully nudified media may bring a district-court
civil action.

Available relief includes:

- compensatory damages;
- mental-anguish or suffering damages;
- up to three times actual damages;
- punitive damages;
- injunctive relief;
- reasonable attorney fees;
- costs and disbursements; and
- other just and equitable relief.

## Attorney-general enforcement

The Minnesota Attorney General may enforce the statute under section 8.31.

A violation may result in a civil penalty not exceeding $500,000 for each
unlawful access, download, or use.

## August 1 distinction

This is a direct private-sector effective date.

It is legally distinct from the August 1 deadline in Executive Order 14409,
which principally requires Federal officials to construct a voluntary
frontier-model consultation and benchmarking framework.
