# Normalized Governance Requirement Taxonomy — Initial Findings

## Constitutional structure

The current legal corpus is not merely a collection of isolated rules.

It resolves into a recurring governance architecture:

1. identify the responsible actor;
2. classify the governed system or capability;
3. determine applicable authority;
4. evaluate risk or capability;
5. control access or execution;
6. preserve evidence and documentation;
7. protect affected people and systems;
8. create audit or review pathways;
9. enforce violations;
10. define scope, limits, and exceptions.

## Federal pattern

Executive Order 14409 is concentrated in:

- government implementation;
- cybersecurity;
- testing and evaluation;
- system classification;
- voluntary coordination;
- authority limitation;
- access protection.

Its August 1 provisions are therefore best understood as creation of Federal
governance infrastructure rather than a universal private-company compliance
deadline.

## Minnesota pattern

Minnesota HF 1606 is concentrated in:

- prohibited capability access;
- execution restriction;
- identity and intimate-representation protection;
- scope and exemption;
- public enforcement;
- private civil action;
- damages and civil penalties.

It represents direct capability-level prohibition rather than generalized AI
risk management.

## Research consequence

These two sources demonstrate why AI governance cannot be represented through
a single category such as transparency, safety, or compliance.

The operative architecture spans authority, classification, evidence,
execution, security, human protection, assurance, enforcement, and scope.
