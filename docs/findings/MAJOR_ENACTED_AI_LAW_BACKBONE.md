# Major Enacted AI-Law Backbone

## Scope

This registry is a selected enacted-law backbone.

It does not yet represent every United States artificial-intelligence law.

The selected measures were included because they establish materially
different governance models suitable for architectural comparison.

## Included governance models

### Federal frontier-model and cybersecurity framework

Executive Order 14409 establishes Federal duties concerning cyber defense,
frontier-model benchmarking, model classification, and voluntary engagement
with developers.

### Comprehensive high-risk AI governance

Colorado SB 24-205 imposes risk-management, documentation, disclosure, and
anti-discrimination duties on developers and deployers of covered high-risk
systems.

### Broad prohibited-use governance

Texas HB 149 establishes prohibited uses, state enforcement, a regulatory
sandbox, and an Artificial Intelligence Council.

### Disclosure and regulatory-learning governance

Utah SB 149 combines AI disclosure duties, consumer-protection enforcement,
and a formal regulatory-learning program.

### Generative-content transparency

California SB 942 focuses on detection, provenance, latent and manifest
disclosures, and transparency for AI-generated content.

### Employment decision governance

Illinois Public Act 103-0804 prohibits employment AI that produces
discriminatory effects and requires employee notice.

### Identity and digital-replica protection

Tennessee Public Chapter 588 extends protection for voice, likeness, and image
against unauthorized synthetic replication.

### Capability prohibition

Minnesota Chapter 72 directly prohibits access to covered nudification
technology and establishes substantial public and private remedies.

## Research significance

These laws do not use one governance model.

Together they reveal at least eight distinct approaches:

1. Federal implementation architecture
2. Frontier-model evaluation
3. High-risk-system risk management
4. Prohibited-use regulation
5. Regulatory sandboxes
6. Transparency and provenance
7. Civil-rights and employment protection
8. Capability-specific prohibition and identity protection
